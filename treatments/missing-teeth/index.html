Implant, bridge or denture: replacing a missing tooth

A missing tooth can be replaced with a dental implant, a bridge or a denture. Implants are fixed in the jaw, bridges are fixed to the teeth beside the gap, and dentures are removable. The right choice depends on your teeth, your gums, your health and your budget.

Replacing missing teeth in South Mumbai: implants, bridges and dentures compared.

Losing a tooth affects more than your smile. The teeth either side can drift into the space, the opposite tooth can over-erupt, and chewing on one side puts extra load on the rest of your teeth. Replacing teeth is the heart of prosthodontics, Dr. Nikhil V. Jain's specialty, so we look at the whole bite before recommending an option. At your consultation we examine the gap and the teeth around it, take any X-rays needed, and explain what each option would involve for you.

about

What are the options for missing teeth?

Dental implant

A titanium post in the jaw holds a crown. It stands on its own, so the neighbouring teeth are not touched. It needs enough healthy bone and a healing period of some months before the final tooth is fitted.

Bridge

A false tooth is held by crowns on the teeth either side of the gap. It is fixed and usually quicker than an implant, but the supporting teeth are shaped to take the crowns.

Denture

A removable plate with one or more teeth. It is the least invasive and usually the least costly, and it can replace many teeth at once, but it feels less like your own teeth and comes out for cleaning.

benefits

Why we recommend replacing a missing tooth
  • It keeps the neighbouring and opposite teeth from moving out of position.
  • It spreads the load of chewing evenly, protecting your other teeth.
  • It restores your smile and, for front teeth, the way you speak.
who is it for?

Which option am I a candidate for?
  • An implant often suits a single missing tooth with healthy teeth either side, when the bone and your general health allow.
  • A bridge can suit a gap next to teeth that already need crowns.
  • A denture can suit several missing teeth, or people who prefer to avoid surgery.

Smoking, uncontrolled diabetes and gum disease affect every option, so we check these first.

process

Explaining your consultation
  1. Examination. We look at the gap, the teeth either side, your gums and your bite.
  2. X-rays. To check the bone and the roots of the neighbouring teeth.
  3. Options and estimate. We explain what each option involves, how long it takes, and its cost.
  4. Your decision. You take the time you need; nothing starts until you are ready.
aftercare

Caring for your replacement tooth

Implants and bridges are cleaned like natural teeth, with extra care at the gum line: we show you how to clean under a bridge and around an implant with floss threaders or small brushes. Dentures come out every day for cleaning. Whatever you choose, regular check-ups help it last.

faqs

Find answers to popular questions about missing teeth

Is an implant better than a bridge?

Neither is better for everyone. An implant leaves the neighbouring teeth untouched; a bridge is quicker and needs no surgery. We recommend what suits your teeth after an examination.

How soon after an extraction can a tooth be replaced?

It depends on the option and on how the area heals. Sometimes a temporary tooth can be fitted straight away; implants usually wait for some healing. We plan the timing with you.

What if I leave the gap?

Some gaps, especially at the very back, cause few problems. Others let teeth tip and drift, which can affect your bite. We explain what is likely in your case.
