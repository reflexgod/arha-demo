A tooth that feels like your own

An implant is a small post, usually titanium, that takes the place of a missing tooth’s root. Once it has settled into the jaw, a crown, bridge or denture is fixed on top.

At Arha the tooth you will see and use is planned first, and the implant is placed to support it. The part above the gum is designed as carefully as the part below.

Who implants are for

  • One missing tooth

    A single implant and crown, without reshaping the healthy teeth on either side.

  • Several missing teeth

    Implants can support a bridge, so fewer implants replace more teeth.

  • Loose or uncomfortable dentures

    A denture or a fixed bridge can be anchored to implants so it stays firmly in place.

  • A tooth that cannot be saved

    When a tooth has to come out, its replacement can be planned at the same time.

Not everyone is suited to implants straight away. Your gums, your bone and your general health all matter, and a consultation is the only way to know.

The implant journey

  1. 01

    Consultation

    We listen to what you want and examine your mouth, with the X-rays or scans needed to plan safely. You leave with a clear explanation of your options.

  2. 02

    Treatment planning

    The final tooth is designed first, then the implant position is planned around it, so appearance and bite are decided before anything is placed.

  3. 03

    Implant placement

    The implant is placed at a pace that keeps you comfortable. We will explain beforehand how your smile will look and work while you heal.

  4. 04

    Healing

    The bone gradually bonds with the implant. We review how it is settling and keep you comfortable in the meantime.

  5. 05

    Your final tooth

    The crown, bridge or denture is made to match your smile and fitted with your bite checked carefully, so it looks and feels natural.

  6. 06

    Aftercare

    Regular reviews and cleanings protect the implant and the gum around it, just as they protect your natural teeth.

Time involved

Implant treatment happens in stages, with healing time in between. How long it takes depends on your bone, your general health and how many teeth are involved, and you will receive a personal timeline at your consultation.
