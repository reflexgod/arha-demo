Go to a hospital first if

  • The swelling is spreading towards your eye or down your neck
  • You find it hard to breathe or swallow
  • You have a high fever along with a swollen face
  • Bleeding is heavy and you feel faint or dizzy
  • You have hurt your head or jaw, or your jaw will not close properly

Go to the nearest hospital emergency department, or call 112. Call us once you are safe.

What counts as a dental emergency

If any of these is happening, call us today. Don’t wait for it to settle.

  • Severe painA toothache that keeps you awake or painkillers don’t touch
  • SwellingA swollen face, jaw or gum, often with a bad taste
  • Bleeding that won’t stopAfter an extraction, an injury or a fall
  • A broken toothChipped, cracked or broken, with or without pain
  • A knocked-out toothMinutes matter for an adult tooth

First aid while you wait

Simple steps that help until you reach us.

Severe toothache

  1. Rinse your mouth with warm water.
  2. Floss gently around the tooth. Trapped food is sometimes the cause.
  3. Hold a cold pack wrapped in a cloth against your cheek, 15 minutes at a time.
  4. Take a painkiller you have taken safely before, as the pack says.

Don’t put aspirin or any tablet on the tooth or gum. It burns the gum.

Call us even if the pain eases. Pain that comes and goes can still mean an infection.

Swelling

  1. Hold a cold pack wrapped in a cloth against the outside of your cheek, 15 minutes on, 15 minutes off.
  2. Keep your head raised on an extra pillow when you lie down.
  3. Keep sipping water.

Don’t put heat on the swelling, and don’t press or burst it.

Call us the same day. Swelling usually means infection, and it needs to be seen. If it spreads towards your eye or neck, go to a hospital.

Bleeding that won’t stop

  1. Fold clean gauze or a clean cloth into a thick pad. A damp tea bag works too.
  2. Place it on the spot and bite down firmly for 20 minutes without checking.
  3. Sit upright and stay calm. Repeat once with a fresh pad if needed.

Don’t rinse, spit or drink through a straw. It washes away the clot.

A little blood in your saliva for a day after an extraction is normal. If it is still bleeding steadily after two rounds of pressure, call us.

A broken or chipped tooth

  1. Rinse your mouth with warm water.
  2. Keep any broken pieces in a little milk and bring them with you.
  3. Chew on the other side, and avoid very hot, cold or hard food.
  4. If a sharp edge cuts your tongue or cheek, cover it with a small piece of sugar-free chewing gum.

Call us. A tooth that hurts when you bite, or with cold, should be seen soon.

A knocked-out tooth

An adult tooth: act within minutes

  1. Pick it up by the crown, the white part you chew with. Don’t touch the root.
  2. If it is dirty, rinse it in milk or cold water for a few seconds. Don’t scrub it or wrap it in tissue.
  3. Push it gently back into its socket, the right way round, and bite on a clean cloth to hold it.
  4. If it won’t go back, keep it in a cup of milk.

Reach a dentist within 30 to 60 minutes. Call us on the way.

A baby (milk) tooth

  1. Don’t put it back. It can harm the adult tooth growing underneath.
  2. Press a clean cloth on the gum if it is bleeding.
  3. Bring your child in so we can check the gum and the teeth around it.
