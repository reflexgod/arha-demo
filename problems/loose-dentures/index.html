What we fix

Loose or uncomfortable dentures

A denture that moves when you eat or speak can be held firmly by a few implants. Sometimes a refit or a new denture is enough.

Book a consultationCall now

What you may notice

  • Movement when you eat

    The denture lifts or rocks when you chew.

  • Sore spots

    Places on the gums that hurt where the denture presses.

  • Avoiding foods

    Leaving out foods you used to enjoy.

  • Speech

    Clicking, or the denture slipping, when you talk.

Conventional or implant-retained?

Choose one to see it, and the three main differences.

Conventional

What holds it
The fit against the gums, and sometimes adhesive.
When you eat and talk
It can lift or rock, especially a lower denture.
Over the years
Refitted or remade as the gums change.

Implant-retained

What holds it
Two or more implants it clips onto.
When you eat and talk
It stays firmly in place.
Over the years
The implants help preserve the bone beneath.

How we usually fix it

The options, side by side. Which suits you is decided together, after an examination.

  • A refit or new denture

    The fit is adjusted to how your gums have changed, or a new denture is made.

    Full mouth rehabilitation

  • Implant-retained denture

    Two or more implants hold the denture firmly; you still take it out to clean.

    Dental implants

  • Fixed implant teeth

    A full arch of teeth fixed to implants, for some patients.

What to expect

Visits and timing are decided at your consultation.

  1. 01

    A conversation

    You tell us what is bothering you and what you would like. We listen before we look.

  2. 02

    An examination

    A careful look at the teeth, gums and bite, with X-rays if they are needed.

  3. 03

    Your options, explained

    What each option involves, how it would look and feel, and what it costs, so you can decide calmly.

  4. 04

    A plan made for you

    The number of visits and the timing are agreed with you at the consultation, never guessed in advance.
