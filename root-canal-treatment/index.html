Root canal treatment

Saving a tooth whose nerve is infected or inflamed.

Book a Consultation Call +91 00000 00000

Keep your own tooth

Inside each tooth is the pulp: nerves and blood vessels in narrow canals. When decay or a crack lets infection reach it, root canal treatment removes the infected pulp and seals the canals.

At Arha the canals are cleaned with rotary instruments: fine, flexible files driven by a motor. The tooth is then usually protected with a crown.

Is this for you?

  • Lingering pain or sensitivity Pain that stays after hot or cold, or wakes you at night.
  • A deep cavity or crack Damage that reaches close to the nerve.
  • Swelling or a gum boil Signs that infection has spread from the tooth.
  • A tooth that has darkened Often after an old knock or injury.

What happens, step by step

Step through a root canal treatment, then check the common signs.

01

Step 1 of 4

Infected pulp

Decay or a crack has let bacteria reach the pulp, causing pain, swelling or sensitivity that lingers.

02

Step 2 of 4

Cleaned

Under local anaesthetic, the infected pulp is removed and the canals are cleaned and shaped with rotary instruments.

03

Step 3 of 4

Sealed and checked

The clean canals are filled and sealed so that bacteria cannot return, and an X-ray checks the result.

04

Step 4 of 4

Crown on top

A crown is usually placed to protect the tooth, as a treated back tooth is more brittle.

Questions, answered

It is done under local anaesthetic, and most people find it feels like having a filling. It relieves the pain the infection was causing.

It depends on the tooth and the infection. We will tell you before we start.

Back teeth usually do, because a treated tooth is more brittle. Front teeth sometimes need only a filling.

Keeping your own tooth is usually better for chewing and for the teeth around it. If a tooth cannot be saved, we will explain the replacements.
