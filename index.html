Common problems we fix

Choose what is bothering you to see how it is usually put right.

A missing tooth

A gap affects how you chew and, over time, how the teeth beside it sit.

It can be replaced with an implant, a bridge or a denture, depending on your gums, your bone and the teeth around it.

Chipped or broken tooth

Small chips can often be rebuilt with tooth-coloured bonding.

A larger break may need a crown to protect what is left of the tooth.

Cosmetic dentistry

Also: Crowns & bridges

Gaps between teeth

Gaps can be closed by moving the teeth, or by adding to them with bonding or veneers.

The right choice depends on the size of the gap and on your bite.

Invisalign clear aligners

Crooked teeth

Teeth can be straightened at almost any age, with clear aligners or with braces.

Straighter teeth are also easier to keep clean.

Invisalign clear aligners

Also: Orthodontics (braces)

Yellow or stained teeth

Surface stains often lift with a professional clean. Deeper colour responds to whitening.

Where whitening cannot help, bonding or veneers can.

Toothache or sensitivity

Sensitivity can come from worn enamel or receding gums. Pain that lingers often means decay has reached the nerve.

An exam and an X-ray tell which it is. A filling or root canal treatment can usually save the tooth.

Root canal treatment

Bleeding gums

Gums that bleed when you brush are usually inflamed, and that is treatable.

A professional clean and better care at home often settle it. Left alone, it can loosen teeth.

Old metal fillings

Old metal fillings can crack, or leak at the edges, over the years.

When one needs replacing, it can be redone in tooth-coloured material, or with a crown.

Loose or uncomfortable dentures

A denture that moves when you eat or speak can be held firmly by a few implants.

Sometimes a refit or a new denture is enough.

My child’s teeth

Baby teeth hold space for the adult teeth and matter for eating and speech.

Regular checks, sealants and fluoride help stop decay early.
